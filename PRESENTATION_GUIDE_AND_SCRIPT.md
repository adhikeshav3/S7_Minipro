### Slide 3: Literature Review
> **Speaker Script:**  
> *"In our literature review, we evaluated four distinct categories of grievance and dispute systems:  
> 1. **National portals like CPGRAMS and UGC e-Samadhan**: While great at centralized ingestion, they suffer from slow, multi-week human routing and lack automated severity prioritization.  
> 2. **Commercial helpdesk platforms like Zendesk or Jira**: Excellent for general IT support, but fundamentally misaligned with academic statutory law—they have no concept of an independent university ombudsperson or re-evaluation ordinances.  
> 3. **Automated Dispute Resolution (ADR) systems**: Recent research shows that deterministic, rule-based NLP triage provides the legal auditability and reliability that universities demand.  
> 4. **Whistleblower protection studies**: Research emphasizes that fear of academic victimisation prevents reporting in severe harassment cases unless identity masking is mathematically guaranteed."*

### Slide 4: Findings from the Review (Research Gaps)
> **Speaker Script:**  
> *"From this literature review, we identified three critical technical gaps that shaped our project:  
> - **Gap 1: Absence of Policy Awareness**: Existing portals treat complaints as plain text strings without knowing what bylaws apply. Our system automatically matches petitions against university statutes like Ordinance 14-B.  
> - **Gap 2: Missing Priority & SLA Triage**: Conventional systems follow First-In, First-Out (FIFO). A student debarred from an exam tomorrow waits behind someone asking for a duplicate ID card. Our triage engine scores urgency and enforces 24-hour turnaround for critical cases.  
> - **Gap 3: Informal Comments instead of Binding Decrees**: Typical portals leave plain chat comments like 'Resolved'. In our system, the engine formulates a certified legal decree with numbered directives, compliance dates, and authorized digital signatures."*

## ❓ Frequently Asked Viva Questions & Model Answers

### Q1: "Why did you avoid using an LLM / ChatGPT API directly in the interface?"
> **Answer:**  
> *"In statutory institutional environments, consumer LLMs present three critical risks: **hallucination of non-existent regulations**, **non-deterministic outputs**, and **severe privacy concerns** with student data.  
> For legal dispute resolution under UGC regulations, decisions must be 100% deterministic, explainable, and auditable. Our system uses a curated institutional NLP lexicon and weighted scoring matrix that maps directly to codified university statutes with zero hallucination risk."*

### Q2: "How does the system ensure whistleblower anonymity?"
> **Answer:**  
> *"When a student toggles the Whistleblower Shield, the intake subsystem tokenizes the identity as `PROTECTED-STUDENT-***`. The student's legal name, roll number, and phone are redacted from all department-facing views. The student tracks the case and receives hearing summons exclusively through an encrypted 4-digit PIN and docket token, guaranteeing protection against academic victimisation."*

### Q3: "What happens if a committee officer does not resolve a grievance within the SLA?"
> **Answer:**  
> *"The SLA Watchdog continuously computes remaining hours against the statutory deadline. When a deadline expires, the system automatically flags the docket in red as 'SLA Breached' and triggers an auto-escalation alert directly to the Vice-Chancellor's Secretariat and the Independent University Ombudsperson."*
